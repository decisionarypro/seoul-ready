THE FIRST-TIMER FIELD GUIDE

Your first 48 hours in Seoul, made simpler.

After a flight, you need a few good decisions—not a list of 40 things to see. Start with the essentials and leave room for a real trip.

Step 1 — Know your airport and where you're staying

First, confirm whether your flight arrives at Incheon International Airport (ICN) or Gimpo International Airport (GMP). These are different airports. Save your accommodation name, address in Korean, nearest subway station and check-in policy in advance.

From Incheon, typical choices include the Airport Railroad (AREX), an airport bus, or an officially designated taxi. Compare the journey to your exact accommodation—not just “to Seoul”—including luggage, transfers and the time you land. For current train and bus options, start with the official Incheon Airport transportation guide.

Useful arrival rule: When tired, choose a transfer you can explain in one sentence. The cheapest combination of three transfers may not be the best first impression of Seoul.

Step 2 — Get connected before leaving the terminal

If you purchased an eSIM, connect and test maps or messaging according to the provider's activation instructions. If your phone is incompatible, local tourist SIMs and other connectivity options are sold at airport or retail locations. Do not assume data-only products include a usable Korean voice/SMS number.

Consider installing NAVER Map or KakaoMap before departure. Korea Tourism Organization lists both as useful route and place information tools for international visitors.

Step 3 — Learn one transit habit

Seoul's subway connects many major districts. A transport card such as T-money can simplify journeys, and single-journey tickets are another option. Where available, follow station instructions about tapping in and out. The official Visit Seoul transportation page has up-to-date guidance on subway and bus fares.

For a taxi at Incheon Airport, use official taxi stands and check the current airport guidance. Do not accept unsolicited offers from people approaching you outside designated taxi areas.

Day one — Keep your first plan deliberately light

After arriving and checking in, pick one nearby neighborhood, an easy meal and a short walk. Avoid scheduling a tightly timed paid tour immediately after an international flight, because immigration, transport and check-in can be unpredictable.

  • If you're staying around Myeongdong: explore the area on foot and keep dinner flexible.
  • If you're staying around Hongdae: explore a nearby cafe street or independent shops.
  • If you're staying near Gwanghwamun: get your bearings around the central historic district.

Day two — Choose one theme, not six attractions

Pick a theme you actually care about. Travelers interested in history might focus on a palace and surrounding streets. Food lovers might devote several hours to markets and neighborhoods. For a first visit, a guided city introduction may help you understand the geography before exploring independently.

Five mistakes worth avoiding

  1. Planning your transfer without checking which airport you're landing at.
  2. Assuming an eSIM automatically includes a local Korean phone number.
  3. Checking hotel distance in kilometers instead of actual public transit connections.
  4. Buying several fixed-time activities before accounting for travel fatigue.
  5. Assuming prices and operating times in old blog posts are still accurate.
